I've been building Stash Squirrel in the open for most of a year, writing up each piece as it landed, without ever really telling anyone it existed. That felt right while there were sharp edges. There are fewer now, so this is me saying hello properly.

## What it actually is

A list in Stash Squirrel can hold **todos, bookmarks and notes at the same time**.

That sounds small until you notice how often it isn't. A side project has a ship checklist, three links you keep losing, some half-formed notes about tone of voice, and a launch date. That's four apps, and the thing that ties them together — the thing that makes them *your project* — only exists in your head.

Most tools make you pick a shape first. Tasks go here, links go there, documents somewhere else. Stash Squirrel lets the list be the project, and the project hold whatever it needs.

It sits in a gap I kept falling into: too small for Notion, too organised for a Google Doc.

## What's landed since the summer

The [last post](/blog/live-on-the-chrome-web-store) was the browser extension reaching the Chrome Web Store in July. Since then:

- **Shared lists.** Invite someone by email and you're both working on the same live list — not a copy. They can add and edit, or just look, and changes appear for everyone without a refresh. Sharing is a Pro feature; being invited to one is free.
- **Discover grew up.** The community catalogue now has hearts, so good lists rise, and a *Most liked* sort. There's a report button, and the tools behind it, because a public catalogue without a way to take something down is an accident waiting to happen.
- **Take your data with you.** One button on the account page gives you every list, item, setting and date as a JSON file. No subscription needed, including if yours has lapsed. Being able to leave isn't a paid feature.
- **The welcome tour finally knows what the app does.** It had been quietly out of date for months, cheerfully failing to mention half of what had shipped.

## Come and poke at it

There's a **demo button on the home page**. One click puts you in a real, pre-loaded account for thirty minutes — no signup, no card, nothing to uninstall afterwards. Everything works: add things, drag them around, break something. If you decide you like it, you can sign up before the session ends and keep what you made.

The free plan is three lists and fifty items, which is enough to find out whether the idea suits you. Pro is £6 a month and lifts the caps, and adds events and a calendar that spans every list, search across everything, sharing, and Discover.

## The ask

If you try it, I'd like to know what you think — particularly the unflattering parts. What felt clumsy, what you expected to be there and wasn't, what made you close the tab. One person built this, so there's no committee to route feedback through; it comes straight to me and I read all of it.

There's a feedback link on every post. I'd genuinely rather hear that something is wrong than hear nothing at all.
